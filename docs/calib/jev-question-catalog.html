What we take from Ms. Thư's research

Her branch uses Jev as Noul gates that stop bad data before it is bought or counted (keyword belongs, question in country scope, landing belongs), behind a separate judgment seam, with every probability kept. Measured on the Japan export: 52% of question landings hit transit, 34% of the rest landed on the wrong entity, 10.6% of questions were about another country, and generic keywords such as hot springs (368k/mo) were bought for one named onsen.

1. Rules first, judgments second

Anything a free rule can decide runs before Jev and shrinks what Jev is asked. Her ticket 01 (transit stays out of the match index) removed 52% of pairs at no cost. Adopt: we build the transit rule before the landing question (A3-5).

2. Several asks in one request

Her seam's contract: independent questions over one state travel together, so two answers cost about one. Keywords of one place go in a single call, keyed by keyword. Adopt: our decide() sends one subject per call today; add a batch form (one state, N keyed asks) before the high-volume stages.

3. Judge once, store beside the subject

A question is foreign once and stays foreign; she stores the verdict keyed on the question and the country set it was judged against, and a wider country set re-opens it. Adopt: a verdict cache keyed on (question, subject, question version); today every call is re-asked.

4. The floor follows the costlier mistake

Keyword gate and landing gate: a false reject is invisible later (never bought, never counted), so the floor sits low (0.15). Question scope: a foreign question left in is a vote in the score, so the floor is 0.5. Adopt: each question in this catalog states which error is costlier.

5. Keep every judgment, delete nothing

Her ADR 0023: a gate flags rather than blocks; a rejected landing keeps its row and stops counting. Adopt, with one change: we act on confident verdicts (enforce), but "act" means exclude from a purchase or a count, never delete data. The ledger (decision_log) keeps every probability.

6. Where Jev must not go

Not in score (a rank-sum with nothing to tune), not in atom extraction, not in any prose writer, and judges only as a prefilter, because a judge's value is its quoted evidence. Adopt as is.

7. Version the wording

A probability moves when the wording moves; her judge carries a signature so a stored answer from other wording is not reused. Adopt: add a question-wording hash to decision_log and to the cache key.

8. Ask the human the same question

Her blind check of 60 landings agreed with Jev only 51%, one-directionally; the likely cause was a human question different from Jev's. Adopted in our first calibration: one operational meaning written down first (see the code list in the keyword record). Result: 67/69 agreement on review.

To settle with Ms. Thư

  1. Keyword floor: 0.30 or 0.15? Ours is from 200 labelled pairs (55/55 correct at ≤ 0.30); hers from 1,070 unlabelled keywords, with false rejects at 0.42–0.50 and hot springs at 0.17. Proposal: keep 0.30 and read the rejects of the first enforced runs on /admin/decisions; drop toward 0.15 at the first false reject.
  2. Generic questions are out, platform-wide? Her marketer decision: a question that names nowhere and fits any country ("What do Buddhists eat?") stops counting. Proposal: adopt for all countries (it is why her strict rule exists).
  3. Country scope: the export or the tour? Her scope is the export's country set (Japan only). We run 21 countries in one pool, so "about Vietnam" is in scope for the platform but not for a Bhutan Segment. Proposal: judge against the Segment's own tour countries, stored with that set.
  4. Landing question: re-adjudicate first. Her 51% agreement leaves the floor unset. Proposal: we label only same-entity-type pairs (temple↔temple), ~200, with the question written as Jev's, before any floor.
  5. Flag vs act. Her ADR 0023 never blocks; we act on confident verdicts only, never delete. Agree which stages may act (proposal: purchases and counts yes; published content no).
  6. Batch asks. Adopt her "several asks per request" contract in our seam before the landing and T10 questions go live.
  7. Specific keyword, imperfect place. She notes tokyo hotel claimed by Karuizawa to Tokyo is a matching problem, not genericness. Proposal: a separate demand-ownership question (A3-6) instead of loading it onto the keyword gate.
  8. Master-content grounding (new on our side). S1 has no check that the rewrite is supported by the source; her repo writes from atoms, so it did not need one. Ask whether her write stage checks support sentence by sentence and how.
  9. Tenant content and the DPA. Jev reads tenant content only for AA test tenants until a DPA/ZDR is in place. Who contacts TypeSafe?
