## Problem

AI-written text reaches three audiences, but only one of them is told that AI can be wrong:

* Content studio (portal, AA-Partner `portal/`): already shows `AiContentNotice` at review, publish and export, and Terms §3 covers AI content. This is the reference.
* CRM web (AA-Partner `web/`): AI drafts client replies (`crm_draft_reply` and the other crm\_\* stages). Settings > AI says a draft is a suggestion, but the draft itself carries no notice, and nothing asks staff to re-check prices, dates, visa or safety facts.
* TripPlanner (B2C): travellers read tour descriptions written by the CIS AI pipeline. There is no notice and no statement that prices, itineraries and visa conditions are confirmed only in the official quote.
* CIS admin: AI outputs (rewrites, atoms, drafts) are reviewed by staff. A consistent staff notice is useful there too.

Decision (tech lead, S263): build it once in the UI kit, so every app shows the same notice (consistent-not-one-off), in English. Vietnamese strings are added only where the app already has an i18n table.

## Scope

1. **Kit component** `AiNotice` in the CIS canonical kit (`apps/AA-CIS-App/frontend/app/_kit/core/`), synced to the other apps with `tools/kit/kit-sync.sh`. Two variants:
   * `staff` (staff / agency users, shown under each AI output): "AI can make mistakes. Check prices, dates, visa and safety information before you send or publish this."
   * `traveller` (public pages): "Some descriptions are written with the help of AI and may contain mistakes. Prices, itineraries and visa conditions are confirmed in your official quote."
     Accessible markup (role="note"), light and dark themes, no layout shift.
2. **CRM web**: show the `staff` notice on every surface that displays an AI draft or AI suggestion (the draft reply panel and any other crm\_\* AI output). Add an EN + VI i18n key. In Settings > AI, add one rule line: "AI can make mistakes: check facts before sending."
3. **Portal**: replace the local `AiContentNotice` with the kit `AiNotice` (keep its current wording as the portal-specific text, or the variant that matches it), so one component serves the whole ecosystem.
4. **TripPlanner**: show the `traveller` notice on pages that show AI-written tour text (tour detail, trip plan or assembly view), plus a short line in the site footer.
5. **CIS admin**: show the `staff` notice on admin views of AI output where a reviewer acts (rewrite/review screens), if they do not already have one.
6. **Terms**:
   * CRM terms (or the agency agreement page in AA-Partner `web/`) gets an "AI-generated content" section modelled on the portal Terms §3: AI drafts can contain mistakes; the agency reviews and sends; AI output is for information only; binding information is the confirmed quote and booking terms.
   * TripPlanner gets a short "Use of AI" section on its terms/legal page (create the page if none exists, linked from the footer): what AI writes, that it can be wrong, and that the quote and booking terms are what binds.
   * Wording says "can contain mistakes" and "for information". It does not claim to exclude liability the law does not let us exclude. Final legal wording is reviewed by the business owner's lawyer later; this issue ships plain, honest text.

## Out of scope

Lawyer review; Booking (Booking shows travellers no AI-written text today); changes to the AI pipeline itself.
