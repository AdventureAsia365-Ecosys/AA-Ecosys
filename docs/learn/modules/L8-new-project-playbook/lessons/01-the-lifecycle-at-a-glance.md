# The lifecycle at a glance

**Objective:** name the nine phases of the new-project playbook, the gate that closes each one and the role
accountable for it, and find where a new project starts on day 1.

The playbook is a thin profile over standards the industry already uses. It does not invent a method: it picks one
phase model, puts a short gate at the end of each phase and gives every phase one accountable person. Three phases
(build, test, release) repeat for every issue, so a live project sits in several phases at the same time.

Read the three sections shown with this lesson:

1. The lifecycle map: the phases in order, their gates and who is accountable for each.
2. The roles table: who is accountable, responsible, consulted or informed per phase, and the other team shapes.
3. The adoption page's day-1 list: what a new project does in its first two sessions.

## Check yourself

- Which gate runs once per issue instead of once per phase, and why has the build phase no exit gate of its own?
- In which phases is the business owner, not the tech lead, accountable, and what do those phases have in common?
- Can an orchestrator agent be accountable for a phase? What changes when the team has several engineers?
